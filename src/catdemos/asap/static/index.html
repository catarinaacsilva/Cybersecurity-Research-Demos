Can a privacy-harmful Android app be spotted from the permissions it asks for? Explore 29,332 real apps and let the ASAP detectors judge any permission profile.

1Move the mouse over the map of apps

Each point of the map is a permission profile; the server decodes the one under the mouse and scores it. Dots are real apps, the background is the detectors' verdict.

How it works Two autoencoders learn to rebuild the permission lists of benign and of malicious training apps (unsupervised, as in ASAP 2.0). An app that the benign autoencoder rebuilds badly but the malicious one rebuilds well gets a high anomaly score. The map itself comes from a third autoencoder with a 2-D bottleneck: any point decodes to a permission list. The A–E label combines the detector's probability (60 %) with the app's PsDC exposure (40 %): requested permissions weighted by tier (direct personal data, behavioural, structural). Data: NATICUSdroid (UCI, CC BY 4.0).
